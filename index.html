02 / FEATURED PROJECT

Combating Digital Piracy Using OSINT: An SOP-Based Case Study

Final Year Project, industry collaboration with Vulsan X Cydef Sdn. Bhd. | Universiti Malaysia Pahang Al-Sultan Abdullah | 2025

Overview

I designed a Standard Operating Procedure for investigating pirated film content across social platforms, covering identification, collection, documentation, analysis, evidence preservation, reporting and follow-up. I applied it to a real case involving a 2025 international film leak, tracking a large volume of infringing listings across all major platforms and measuring how each platform responded to takedown reports.

The problem

Independent creators and small production teams often lack the time, tools and legal resources to track and remove pirated content. Platform reporting tools are built for individual use, give little transparency, and pirated content can stay online for days or weeks after being reported. Existing anti-piracy work depends heavily on manual searching, screenshots and form filling.

My approach

I built a repeatable, evidence-based process that adds structure and automation to the pre-reporting phase while keeping platform reporting itself manual and compliant. It combines semi-automated collection (Apify and basic Python tooling for keyword matching and link logging) with manual OSINT verification (IntelTechniques, Yandex reverse image search, Google Alerts, Social Buzz).

Key findings

  • Enforcement was uneven across platforms.
  • The platform with the largest volume of infringing content had a low removal rate.
  • Identical content got different outcomes on the same platform.
  • Automated moderation often misses content from small or independent rights holders.
  • Regulatory escalation is a recommended way to strengthen outcomes.

Limitations and future work

Reporting and evidence tracking were largely manual and kept in spreadsheets, which does not scale. I recommended a secure database or dashboard for evidence handling and traceability as future work.
